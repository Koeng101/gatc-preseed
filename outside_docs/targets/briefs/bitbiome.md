# bitBiome

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `enzyme` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: easy · HQ: Tokyo, Japan · Founded: 2018

> AI-driven microbial genome mining platform for enzyme and bioactive compound discovery and biomanufacturing.

## What they do

<cite index="1-1">bitBiome is a data and AI company building the intelligence layer for the next era of biotechnology.</cite> <cite index="2-11,2-12">The platform is built on proprietary single-cell microbial genome analysis technology, bit-MAP®, which has enabled the creation of bit-GEM: an extensive and growing microbial database of over 2.5 billion sequences. Leveraging expertise in bioinformatics, machine learning and AI technologies, the company practices a comprehensive enzyme discovery and enzyme, pathway and proprietary strain engineering platform, bit-QED.</cite>

**AI role**: <cite index="2-12">Machine learning and AI technologies drive comprehensive enzyme discovery and enzyme, pathway and proprietary strain engineering.</cite>

## Why this score

Strong fit on two high-value service lines: (1) clonal DNA <2kb for enzyme variant libraries (high volume, high frequency), and (2) E. coli KRX expression for rapid enzyme characterization and screening. Estimated combined annual demand: 1500-2000 orders across both services, representing 75k-100k USD annual spend at our pricing. Company is early-stage (easy to reach), actively scaling, and has demonstrated willingness to engage commercial partnerships. Primary risk: outsourced CMO manufacturing may eventually reduce need for contract protein production as they move upmarket, but near-term (2-3 yr) demand is strong. Not tier1 because (a) no binder workflow fit, (b) modest headcount likely limits volume ceiling, and (c) geographic distance (Japan) may slow deal closure.

## Risks / concerns

- Still in early-stage funding (Seed extension only, ~22M USD total raised as of 2025); no Series A closed yet despite active fundraising efforts (Hypergrowth Tokyo program participation).
- Heavy reliance on commercial partnerships (Ajinomoto, Tojo Vikas, CMOs) for revenue; own manufacturing capacity being developed but currently outsourced; may face margin pressure or partnership termination risk.
- Bioeconomy and sustainability-focused markets can be subject to commodity price volatility and regulatory changes in biomanufacturing regulations.
- Competition from larger synthetic biology platforms (Ginkgo, Zymergen successor entities) and established industrial biotech vendors with deeper pockets for fermentation/manufacturing R&D.
- Database-driven discovery model depends on sustained data quality, annotation accuracy, and protection of proprietary microbial sequences; risk of IP challenges or data licensing disputes.
- Go-to-market primarily B2B2C (B2B partnerships into consumer goods/pharma); long sales cycles and customer concentration risk.
- Japan-based headquarters may limit proximity to US biotech ecosystem and investor base, though team is globally distributed and actively seeking international expansion.
