# Colossal Biosciences

**Score: 3/5** · (Tier 3 — Cold) · `enzyme` · Stage: series_C_plus · Outreach: hard · HQ: Dallas, United States · Founded: 2021

> CRISPR-based de-extinction and conservation company using multiplex gene editing to revive extinct species and protect endangered animals.

## What they do

Colossal applies CRISPR and advanced genome engineering to de-extinction, developing technologies for extinct species restoration (woolly mammoth, Tasmanian tiger, dodo, dire wolf), critically endangered species protection, and ecosystem repopulation. The company combines genome sequencing, multiplex gene editing, artificial reproductive technologies (artificial wombs), biobanking, and computational biology platforms to build an end-to-end de-extinction pipeline. Beyond core conservation efforts, Colossal spins off commercial entities (Form Bio, Breaking, undisclosed stealth platform) targeting adjacent markets in human healthcare, synthetic biology, and bio-manufacturing.

**AI role**: AI and machine learning are central to Colossal's platform: (1) genomic data analytics for mapping genotype-to-phenotype in extinct vs. living species; (2) computational analysis of ancient DNA assembly and variant prioritization; (3) Form Bio—a spun-out computational biology platform using AI for bioinformatics and dataset management; (4) NVIDIA Parabricks-powered genome analysis delivering 12x faster sequencing at 1/4 cost; (5) AI-powered bird call detection for locating lost species. Church and Hysolli emphasize computational biology as foundational to design of multiplex edits.

## Why this score

Colossal is a strong fit on paper (high DNA synthesis demand, bacterial expression for validation, potential binder work) but scores tier3 due to: (1) Stage & procurement friction (Series C+ / $10B+ valuation = hard reach, likely locked vendor relationships); (2) Unknown in-house synthesis capability (ViaGen acquisition suggests possible vertical integration); (3) No published evidence of external clonal DNA synthesis spend or vendor preferences; (4) Competition from incumbents (Twist, IDT, GenScript) with existing relationships. Positive signals: (1) Rapid growth trajectory (170 scientists, 17 labs) means volume increasing; (2) Active hiring in wet lab roles suggests pipeline expansion; (3) Multiplex editing approach (many constructs per cycle) = high throughput demand; (4) Three-year horizon to de-extinction milestones (2028) = sustained R&D spend. Upside: If clonal DNA <$50 per assembly and 1-week turnaround can be matched, Colossal might trial for high-volume screening or variant libraries.

## Risks / concerns

- Scientific controversy: Independent experts (Jeremy Austin, Vincent Lynch, Nic Rawlence, others) dispute Colossal's de-extinction claims, arguing woolly mice and dire wolves are genome engineering, not true de-extinction. IUCN SSC disputes conservation utility of dire wolf proxies. STAT/Nature reporting (July 2025) revealed coordinated smear campaign targeting academic critics with AI-generated blog posts and bogus YouTube copyright strikes—Colossal denied involvement but reputational damage significant.
- IP and ethical minefield: Patent applications on woolly mammoth genetic combinations face legal/ethical challenges. Uncertain enforceability for wild animals returned to ecosystems. Precedent unclear on patenting extinct DNA or organism phenotypes. Could trigger regulatory backlash if monopoly over de-extinction claimed.
- Execution risk: No living woolly mammoth or Tasmanian tiger yet; timelines aspirational (mammoth by 2028, thylacine, dodo timeline uncertain). Large-scale reproductive biology in non-model organisms is notoriously difficult. Surrogate availability/welfare (e.g., elephants for mammoth, marsupials for thylacine) is bottleneck. Long development cycles.
- Regulatory uncertainty: No regulatory pathway exists for releasing gene-edited extinct species into wild. Government/environmental sign-off required but precedent lacking. Potential backlash from conservation establishment if species are seen as commodified.
- Reputational and perception risk: High-profile failures (e.g., mammoth stillbirth, low surrogate success rates, welfare concerns) would be heavily scrutinized. Public perception of 'playing God' remains sensitive. Academic credibility erosion if dire wolf / woolly mouse claims not substantiated.
- Capital intensity: Requires sustained $100M+ annual burn to maintain global lab network, personnel, R&D. IPO or continuous VC funding essential. If capital dries up, project continuity at risk.
- Technology commoditization: Form Bio, Breaking, artificial womb spinoffs must achieve independent product-market fit and profitability. If spinoffs underperform, revenue diversification fails and company faces deeper dependence on conservation grants and speculation.
- Team / talent risk: Heavily reliant on George Church, Beth Shapiro, Andrew Pask, and Eriona Hysolli. Loss of key scientist to competing group or burnout would degrade execution. Lab culture sustainability with 170+ rapid growth uncertain.
- Regulatory scrutiny on CRISPR germline editing: If Colossal is perceived as experimenting with heritable human germline edits (not stated as goal but possible adjacency), regulatory blowback likely. Reputational association with He Jiankui 'designer baby' scandal risk.
- Funding valuation bubble: $10.3B valuation with zero revenue and highly speculative timeline is aggressive. If broader VC market corrects or de-extinction tech seen as unlikely to yield commercial de-extinction (only research/conservation tech), down round / write-down risk high.
