# Persephone Biosciences

**Score: 3/5** · (Tier 3 — Cold) · `microbial_strain` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: easy · HQ: San Diego, United States · Founded: 2017

> Synthetic biology platform using AI-driven strain engineering to develop live biotherapeutics and clinical synbiotics targeting microbiome restoration for oncology, infant health, and disease prevention.

## What they do

Persephone Biosciences develops microbiome-based medicines and synbiotics using synthetic biology and AI to engineer live bacterial therapeutics. The company operates two divisions: Persephone Consumer Health (infant probiotics and food-as-medicine supplements) and Persephone Therapeutics (oncology-focused live biotherapeutics). Their core platform involves large-scale patient microbiome sampling, multi-omics analysis, and AI-driven strain selection to create precision-matched probiotic and prebiotic combinations.

**AI role**: AI powers 'microbial avatar' predictive models that simulate how a patient's unique microbiome influences response to cancer immunotherapy and chemotherapy. Machine learning drives biomarker discovery and patient-microbiome phenotyping from large real-world datasets (ARGONAUT cohort).

## Recommended pitch

Persephone is engineering Bifidobacterium and Bacteroides strains at unprecedented scale. Your strain optimization loops—promoter variants, genetic circuits, selection markers, QC controls—likely consume 50+ cloned constructs per candidate per cycle. Our clonal DNA service ($50/assembly, <2kb) is half the cost of Twist/GenScript and includes direct E. coli KRX expression if you want same-day protein for any metabolic validation work. Ginkgo's toolkit is powerful for design, but manufacturing the actual DNA quickly and cheaply is bottleneck. Let's talk about batch assembly pricing for your 2025 strain pipeline. Persephone as a customer reference would also be valuable for us.

## Why this score

Persephone scores moderate because: (1) strong fit on clonal DNA under 2kb (high volume, 1000-10000 annual constructs for strain engineering); (2) excellent company stage for outreach (seed, accessible founders, no vendor lock-in); (3) BUT they have in-house or accessible DNA synthesis via Ginkgo partnership, reducing standalone DNA vendor fit; (4) E. coli expression fit is none (live bacteria modality); (5) No binder/recombinant protein modality; (6) Cell-free expression fit is only medium (niche use case). ## Risks / concerns

- Manufacturing scale-up risk: live biotherapeutics require GMP-grade anaerobic fermentation and quality control; no indication of internal manufacturing at commercial scale
- Regulatory pathway uncertainty: LBP/synbiotic regulatory classification and approval timeline unclear; ARTEMIS and ARTEMIS results still early-stage, not Phase 3 powered efficacy data
- Microbiome variability: individual microbiome composition changes rapidly and is influenced by diet, antibiotics, environment; durability of therapeutic colonization and long-term patient compliance unclear
- Market size for infant synbiotic: OTC supplement market is large but price-sensitive; premium positioning ($X/month) depends on strong clinical and consumer education messaging; reimbursement unlikely
- Capital requirements: advancing ARGONAUT to regulatory readiness and securing manufacturing partnerships likely requires Series A+ funding; only $15.3M raised to date
- Competitive landscape: other microbiome platforms (Eligo, Seres, Synlogic) actively developing LBPs; some competitors have more capital and pharma partnerships
- Dependency on clinical trial data: company valuation and future funding heavily dependent on ARTEMIS and ARGONAUT results publication; any negative findings would impair investor confidence
