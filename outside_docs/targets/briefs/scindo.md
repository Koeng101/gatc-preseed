# Scindo

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `enzyme` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: easy · HQ: London, United Kingdom · Founded: 2020

> Full-stack AI enzyme engineering platform for sustainable food, flavor, cosmetics, and specialty chemicals.

## What they do

<cite index="1-2">Scindo uses AI-powered enzyme discovery and design to create next-generation biocatalysts for sustainable manufacturing, transforming industrial chemistry through unprecedented control over selectivity while reducing energy, waste, and reliance on fossil feedstocks.</cite> <cite index="3-1">The company has developed a full-stack platform that combines advanced artificial intelligence with experimentally validated reaction data to design and optimize enzymes.</cite> <cite index="22-7">Scindo develops enzymes for industry verticals including food and flavorings, cosmetics, and specialty chemicals.</cite>

**AI role**: <cite index="1-4,1-5">The platform powers ML models to capture novel enzyme functions, substrates, and transformations along with physical realities of chemistry and reaction conditions through rich metadata, creating an active learning loop that enables models to uncover true drivers of enzyme specificity and performance.</cite> <cite index="35-9,35-10">Scindo operates a large chemistry screening platform to test enzymes, characterize them, and feed data back into machine learning, closing the loop between real-life results and ML models.</cite>

## Risks / concerns

- Clinical/regulatory pathway unclear: Scindo focuses on industrial enzymes for F&B and cosmetics; unclear if any products face pharmaceutical-grade regulatory hurdles.
- Enzyme scalability: <cite index="28-6">Enzyme adoption has been limited by scalability challenges.</cite> Scindo's unannounced partnership suggests early-stage manufacturing validation.
- Market timing: Enzyme-based sustainable chemistry is nascent; commodity petrochemical pricing remains competitive.
- Team depth: Headcount is ~7 (as of mid-2025); rapid scaling in wet-lab and commercialization will require significant hiring.
- IP/data moat: Platform relies on proprietary datasets and ML models; competitive risk from other AI + synthetic biology teams if validation data becomes commoditized.
