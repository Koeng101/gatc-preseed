# Tessera Therapeutics

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `mRNA_or_RNA` · Stage: series_C_plus · Outreach: moderate · HQ: Cambridge, USA · Founded: 2018

> Tessera develops gene writing™—an RNA/DNA-based genome engineering platform that precisely corrects genetic mutations without viral vectors, targeting monogenic diseases and emerging T-cell therapies.

## What they do

Tessera pioneers Gene Writing™, a novel category of genome engineering that writes therapeutic messages into the genome using engineered mobile genetic elements (MGEs). The platform can make single base-pair changes, insertions/deletions, and write entire genes using RNA-templated or DNA-templated systems, delivered via proprietary lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). This contrasts with CRISPR (which cuts) and gene therapy (which uses viral vectors); Gene Writing enables re-dosability, scalable manufacturing via mRNA, and avoidance of double-strand breaks.

**AI role**: Computational tools and data science infrastructure used to evaluate >100,000 engineered/synthetic MGEs, optimize gene-writer sequences (amino acid and RNA/DNA templates), and accelerate discovery cycles. Seqera platform (workflow orchestration) deployed for large-scale genomic analysis. Heavy emphasis on ML-driven platform optimization rather than AI for target discovery.

## Why this score

Tessera is a strong fit on multiple service lines: (1) Clonal DNA under 2 kb: medium fit (not primary, but useful); (2) E. coli KRX expression: high fit (core to MGE variant optimization cycle); (3) Cell-free expression: high fit (rapid prototyping); (4) Binder workflow: low fit (not primary focus). The company is at Series C+ (procurement friction is moderate but not insurmountable), has strong technical depth, and is actively building out internal wet-lab and manufacturing. They are a high-velocity platform company that could order 1000-10000 protein expressions annually for variant screening. However, they are likely already working with Twist or other majors for DNA synthesis and may have in-house expression capability (unknown). Primary opportunity is positioning as a partner for rapid, cost-effective E. coli expression + purification directly from clonal DNA, enabling accelerated MGE optimization. They also represent a potential long-term strategic partnership for manufacturing scale-up as they move toward IND filing.

## Risks / concerns

- Clinical translation risk: No clinical data yet; TSRA-196 IND expected by end of 2025. Gene Writing in humans is unproven; off-target editing, durability of correction, and immune responses remain to be validated.
- Regulatory pathway uncertainty: Gene Writing is a novel category; FDA precedent is limited. Accelerated approval pathways (Fast Track, Orphan Drug) for TSRA-196 reduce some regulatory risk but clinical efficacy and safety bars may be high.
- Manufacturing scale-up: Company is building in-house LNP manufacturing and targeting operational status by 2024-2025. Any delays or technical issues could constrain program advancement.
- Competitive pressure: CRISPR (Editas, CRISPR Therapeutics, Intellia), base editing (Beam, ADARx), and AAV gene therapy (Ultragenyx, Audentes, others) continue to advance. Success of other platforms could diminish investor interest or limit addressable market.
- Dependency on Regeneron partnership: TSRA-196 (lead program) is now in global co-development with Regeneron; loss or significant setback in that partnership could impact valuation and runway.
- IP landscape: Gene Writing relies on engineered MGEs and retrotransposon biology; freedom-to-operate and patent scope remain to be fully tested. Overlap with university and competitor IP is possible.
- Talent retention: Competitive talent market for synthetic biology and genome engineering; company's headcount ~256 (as of Feb 2026) is growing but needs to sustain R&D velocity and clinical readiness.
