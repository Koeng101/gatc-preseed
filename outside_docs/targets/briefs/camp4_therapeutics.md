# CAMP4 Therapeutics

**Score: 3/5** · (Tier 3 — Cold) · `mRNA_or_RNA` · Stage: public · Outreach: hard · HQ: Cambridge, United States · Founded: 2016

> RNA-targeting therapeutics company using antisense oligonucleotides to upregulate gene expression via regulatory RNA modulation.

## What they do

CAMP4 develops disease-modifying treatments for genetic diseases using proprietary antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) designed to selectively upregulate gene expression by modulating regulatory RNA (regRNA). The company targets the 98% of the genome that does not encode proteins ('dark side of genome'). Their RAP Platform® maps regRNAs and identifies therapeutic candidates for haploinsufficient and recessive partial loss-of-function disorders affecting >1,200 diseases.

**AI role**: The company employs proprietary machine learning algorithms as part of its discovery platform to map regulatory RNAs and predict optimal ASO sequences. AI/computational biology tools enable systematic genome-wide regRNA characterization and drug target selection from large epigenomic datasets.

## Risks / concerns

- Clinical-stage risk: CMP-CPS-001 in early Phase 1; CMP-002 not yet in human trials; regulatory approval pathway for novel ASO modality unproven
- Oligonucleotide manufacturing: ASO therapeutics require GMP-grade oligo production; reliance on external partners (e.g., Ionis, Geron) for manufacturing scale-up creates supply chain dependency
- Competitive landscape: Alnylam (established RNAi player), Ionis (large-cap ASO player), and other regRNA/gene-modulation programs could accelerate; differentiation must hold in clinic
- Regulatory uncertainty: Mechanism of action (regRNA targeting to upregulate expression) is novel; FDA may request additional chemistry, CMC, or mechanistic data beyond standard ASO precedent
- Patent/IP: Core regRNA platform and ASO chemistry discoveries originated from academic labs; freedom-to-operate and patent estate dependent on strong protection
- Financial burn: Pre-revenue clinical biotech with ~$51.8M net loss in 2024; IPO and PIPE funding provide ~$182M runway but multi-year pipeline requires sustained capital
- Rare disease focus: Lead programs (UCD, SYNGAP1) are rare diseases; commercial market size smaller than common indications; reliant on partnerships (GSK, BioMarin) for larger-market programs
- Lack of historical commercial milestones: No approved drugs or marketed products to validate go-to-market execution
