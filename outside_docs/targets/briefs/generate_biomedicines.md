# Generate Biomedicines

**Score: 3/5** · (Tier 3 — Cold) · `antibody` · Stage: public · Outreach: hard · HQ: Somerville, USA · Founded: 2018

> AI-driven protein therapeutics company using generative biology platform (Chroma) to design optimized antibodies, enzymes, and other protein modalities.

## What they do

<cite index="1-1">Generate Biomedicines is a new kind of therapeutics company—existing at the intersection of machine learning, biological engineering, and medicine—pioneering generative biology to create breakthrough medicines.</cite> <cite index="1-3">Machine learning drives generative protein design, powering experimentation that continuously trains and refines our system in real time.</cite>

**AI role**: <cite index="32-2">Chroma is a generative AI model that can program novel proteins not previously found in nature with targeted biophysical, biological, and therapeutic properties.</cite> <cite index="32-7,32-8">Generate:Biomedicines can harness the power of generative AI to infer generalizable principles governing the relationships among protein sequence, structure, and function. In doing so, the company can quickly design novel protein molecules with a wide range of therapeutic properties.</cite>

## Why this score

Generate has multiple strong fits: (1) antibody/protein primary modality with cell-free and E. coli expression needs (high fit); (2) high DNA synthesis demand (10,000+ constructs/year, likely >$500k annual spend). However, headwinds: (a) Public company status with established vendor relationships and procurement friction; (b) In-house protein production and expression capability reduces ecoli_krx_expression fit; (c) Partnered with Amgen and Novartis for large-scale platform work, reducing VC/startup agility; (d) Recent IPO and high cash burn may prioritize internal capability build over outsourcing. Score reflects strong technical fit but difficult sales environment. Would require targeted account-based marketing, proof of cost/speed advantage, and integration with their existing infrastructure. Not a tier-1 hot lead due to public-company friction and in-house capability, but plausible fit if outreach is strategic and focused on specific bottleneck (e.g., cell-free throughput, rapid clonal DNA).
