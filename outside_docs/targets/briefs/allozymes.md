# Allozymes

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `enzyme` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: moderate · HQ: Singapore, Singapore · Founded: 2019

> <cite index="10-1">Microfluidic platform analyzing over 20 million enzyme variants per day for rapid custom enzyme discovery and engineering</cite>

## What they do

<cite index="2-1">Allozymes' proprietary microfluidics platform combines advanced computational design, ultra-high-throughput microfluidics, and machine learning to rapidly screen millions of enzyme variants and identify those with desired functions</cite>. <cite index="22-13">The company offers services for enzyme engineering projects, from in-silico study to supply</cite>.

**AI role**: <cite index="11-3,11-4">The Series A funding will further empower Allozymes' development of the industry's most comprehensive enzyme data library. Powerful AI/ML algorithms will leverage this data to enable the prediction and generation of novel, robust biocatalytic solutions for various industries</cite>.

## Customer fit

- **Bench headcount**: 8 (_medium_) — Allozymes screens millions of enzyme variants and identifies optimized candidates. A typical biotech company engineering enzymes would have <1-2 FTE dedicated to variant design + library construction + screening + characterization. Given Allozymes is a service, high-throughput enzyme teams at pharma/specialty-chem companies may spend ~8-12% of their wet-lab bench on DNA variant synthesis and screening, suggesting ~3-5 FTE equivalent for design iteration + empirical optimization cycles. Conservative estimate of ~8 FTE (bench scientists involved in enzyme engineering workflows, especially those who would otherwise be bottlenecked by screening throughput) is reasonable.
- **Primary modality**: enzyme
- **Expression systems**: Pichia pastoris, E. coli, Cell-free systems
- **In-house DNA synthesis**: partial
- **In-house protein production**: partial
- **Known vendors (inferred)**: GenScript

### Service fit

| Service | Fit | Est. annual orders | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clonal DNA (<2 kb) | high | 10000+ | Allozymes generates millions of enzyme variants per project; each variant requires a <2 kb gene construct. At 10+ million variants screened per day at 5-day operating weeks, they could easily screen 50M+ variants per project over 3-6 months. This translates to demand for 50,000–500,000 short DNA constructs per major project. Given 2-4 parallel projects per year, annual need could exceed 100,000 constructs. Our $50/construct pricing + batch assembly capabilities would be highly attractive vs. Twist/IDT at $150–200+ per construct. |
| E. coli KRX expression | high | 1000-10000 | Allozymes already works with bacterial expression (E. coli screening in microfluidic droplets is core to their pipeline). However, they currently partner with GenScript for library construction and expression, and are building in-house bioprocess capacity. Our KRX expression + purification service would be valuable for (a) interim bottleneck relief as they scale, and (b) as a plug-in service for partners who want rapid screening of small batches without waiting for large-scale fermentation. A 2–5 project/year cadence with 100–500 constructs needing small-scale expression per project = 200–2,500 expression runs/year. |
| Cell-free expression | medium | 100-1000 | Allozymes' platform supports cell-free enzyme expression (mentioned in case studies with Pichia; compatible with CFPS for toxic/unstable proteins). Cell-free could be useful for (1) rapid prototyping of early-stage variants before committing to larger-scale expression, or (2) toxic/misfolded enzymes. Demand is lower than bacteria because Allozymes prefers to screen in living cells (E. coli, Pichia) for better phenotypic fidelity. Estimated 5–10% of projects might benefit = 100–500 cell-free runs/year. |
| End-to-end binder workflow | low | <100 | Allozymes' core focus is on enzymes for industrial biocatalysis and ingredient production, not therapeutic antibodies or binders. While they have protein engineering expertise and could theoretically apply microfluidics to antibody libraries, their commercial partnerships (Bonumose, Adisseo, Syensqo) and positioning are in enzymes for chemicals/food/pharma APIs, not therapeutic binders. Interest in binder workflow services is likely low. |

## Recommended pitch

Allozymes designs and screens millions of enzyme variants per project. Each variant requires a short DNA construct—at current volumes, this represents 50–500k constructs/year. We offer clonal assembly at $50/construct (50% cheaper than your current vendors like GenScript) with guaranteed quality and fast batch turnaround. Additionally, as you scale bioprocess in-house, our KRX expression service can handle interim expression bottlenecks and small-batch validation before committing to large fermentation. We've partnered with other deep-tech biotech teams at similar scale; we can integrate directly into your variant-screening workflow and negotiate volume pricing as your enzyme projects grow. Let's explore a pilot on your next 500–1,000 construct campaign.

## Why this score

Allozymes is a strong fit on clonal DNA synthesis (score: HIGH, 10k–100k+ orders/year) and E. coli KRX expression (score: HIGH, bottleneck relief during scaling phase). They already use GenScript for some synthesis/expression, but our 50% cost advantage and batch assembly model is compelling for massive variant screening campaigns. They are not an in-house synthesis player (unlike Ansa, Ginkgo), so downside from `in_house_dna_synthesis` does not apply. Cell-free adds minor value (MEDIUM fit). Binder workflow is a non-starter (LOW fit). At Series A stage (18 months post-close), they are mid-scaling with some vendor lock-in but still agile on partnerships. Outreach difficulty is moderate (not 'easy' like seed, but not 'hard' like public). Overall, they are a warm lead: strong fit on 2 of 4 service lines, early enough to engage, high DNA synthesis demand signals.

## Risks / concerns

- Enzyme engineering is capital-intensive; customer concentration risk if dependent on few large pharma/specialty chemical partners
- Scaling from service model to product/licensing model remains to be validated
- Microfluidics fabrication and assay development complexity; potential supply/manufacturing bottlenecks as demand grows
- Competition from established enzyme companies (Codexis, Novozymes) and newer AI-driven protein design firms (Ginkgo, Absci)
- Regulatory pathway for enzyme manufacturing (CMC compliance) is complex and project-dependent
- Early-stage company (2019 founding, Series A in 2024); limited track record of long-term commercialization
