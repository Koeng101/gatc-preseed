# 1910 (formerly 1910 Genetics)

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `small_molecule` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: moderate · HQ: Boston, United States · Founded: 2018

> AI-native biotech integrating multimodal AI, wet-lab automation, and biological data to design small molecules and large-molecule therapeutics.

## What they do

1910 designs small-molecule drugs and protein therapeutics (antibodies, macrocyclic peptides, enzymes) using a proprietary ITO™ platform that combines frontier AI models (LLMs, diffusion models, graph neural networks), physics-based simulations, and closed-loop laboratory automation. The company generates three proprietary data streams—computational data (QM/MM, FEP, molecular docking), wet-lab proxy biological data (high-throughput surrogate assays like their Permeability Proxy Assay™), and wet-lab ground truth data (biochemical, cellular, and in vivo assays)—to train and validate AI-designed molecules.

**AI role**: Central. Multimodal AI models (ELVIS™ for small molecules, ROSALYND™ for proteins, PEGASUS™ for macrocyclic peptides, CANDID™ for ADMET prediction, CANDID-CNS™ for blood-brain barrier permeability) are tightly integrated with wet-lab automation and robotics. The company runs closed-loop iterative cycles: AI designs → synthesis → biological assay → data feeds back to retrain models. AI handles hit discovery, lead generation, multi-parameter optimization, and structure prediction; biology validates and generates data at scale.

## Recommended pitch

Pitch should emphasize: (1) **DNA synthesis cost arbitrage**: 1910 designs hundreds of candidates/month; your $50/assembly (vs. $100+) saves 50% on their biggest input cost. With 10K+ constructs/yr, this is $500K+ annual savings. (2) **Turnround speed & integration**: You can clone immediately post-synthesis and feed into their KRX expression platform same-day. Reduces iteration cycle from weeks to days. (3) **Volume commitment alignment**: 1910's business model thrives on iteration velocity—you are not a vendor, you are a throughput accelerator. Offer tiered pricing for volume (e.g., 1K+/yr tiers) and fast turnaround SLA (24–48 hr synthesis + assembly). (4) **Early-stage partnership advantage**: Position as preferred vendor for their internal pipeline + partner programs. Bundle clonal DNA + optional E. coli KRX expression + BLI if they lack in-house throughput. (5) **Avoid the 'software tool' framing**—1910 is hardware + software + wet lab. You are infrastructure, not an app. Emphasize reliability, cost, and speed over UI/UX.

## Risks / concerns

- Stage-gated translation risk: AI can accelerate design iteration, but pharma R&D success depends on in vivo efficacy, safety, manufacturability, and regulatory approval. 1910's molecules still must pass clinical trials; AI prediction gaps in immunogenicity, pharmacokinetics, and cellular context remain.
- Data quality and generalization: Even with proprietary wet-lab data generation, models trained on 1910's specific assay formats may not generalize to all therapeutic targets or disease contexts. Overfitting to in-house assay proxies could limit external partner adoption.
- Vendor lock-in risk for Microsoft partnership: Azure Quantum Elements collaboration ties 1910's infrastructure to Microsoft compute. While providing scale, it reduces optionality for competing HPC providers and creates dependency.
- Early-stage pipeline: 1910's internal pipeline (pain, neurological, autoimmune candidates) is still in discovery/preclinical; no clinical candidates disclosed. Revenue depends on platform licensing and partner programs, not marketed drugs.
- Competitive intensity: Recursive, Insilico, XtalPi, BigHat Biosciences, Exscientia, and others are also combining AI + biology. Differentiation via wet-lab automation is strong but not unique; compute partnerships and pharma relationships will be key to staying ahead.
- Hiring and scaling: Rapid growth (18–33 employees in 24 months) required recruiting top computational chemists, ML engineers, and assay biologists. Boston biotech talent competition is fierce.
- Customer concentration risk: Heavy reliance on pharma partnerships and Microsoft go-to-market collaboration. Loss of key partner or Microsoft relationship could significantly impact revenue growth.
- Regulatory uncertainty: AI-designed therapeutics have limited precedent in FDA evaluation; uncertainty around how regulators will review AI-generated safety/efficacy data and proprietary training datasets.
