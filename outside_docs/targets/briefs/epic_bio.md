# Epic Bio

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 1 — Hot) · `AAV_or_gene_therapy` · Stage: series_B · Outreach: moderate · HQ: South San Francisco, USA · Founded: 2019

> Epigenetic gene-modulation therapy company using ultracompact Cas proteins and modulator libraries to treat haploinsufficient diseases without cutting DNA

## What they do

<cite index="6-7">Epic Bio develops a new class of genetic medicines that act on the epigenome, a naturally occurring mechanism that determines which genes are expressed and at what levels.</cite> <cite index="3-1,3-5,3-6">Their therapies involve fusing together a protein that binds to DNA with a so-called modulator protein that can make epigenetic changes to the DNA molecule, with this construct directed to a target site in the genome using a customized guide RNA molecule.</cite> <cite index="3-7,3-8,3-9">The technology is dubbed Gene Expression Modulation System, or GEMS for short. CRISPR-Cas9 binds and cuts the DNA whereas GEMS binds and modifies the chemistry of DNA without changing the genetic code, allowing fine-tuning gene expression and avoiding the risks of cutting DNA.</cite>

**AI role**: <cite index="7-8">Epic screened hundreds of transcription-activating peptides for robust combinatorial activity, then applied machine learning to predict combinations with the greatest effect on gene expression.</cite> <cite index="22-1">The company has artificial intelligence discovery pipelines to discover and engineer and optimize epigenome editing molecules.</cite> <cite index="2-18,2-19,2-20">Epic Bio formed an AI partnership with Springbok Analytics that uses MRI muscle imaging and natural history data to analyze muscle fat fraction and inflammation images, with the goal of a 'digital twin' concept, offering robust, measurable evidence for regulators.</cite>

## Why this score

Epic Bio is strong fit on 2 of 4 service lines (E. coli KRX expression: high; cell-free expression: high) with genuine demand for rapid, iterative protein screening and engineering cycles driving DNA synthesis and expression orders. Primary modality (AAV gene therapy) typically requires construct lengths >2 kb, so clonal_dna_under_2kb fit is medium rather than high, but the modulator screening/engineering subworkflow creates steady demand for short ORF constructs. Series B stage + active clinical trial + public founder/CEO visibility + explicit need for high-throughput protein engineering stack position this as a warm lead. Not tier1_hot for all-service-lines fit, but tier1_hot for total addressable opportunity (protein discovery + expression + characterization) and accessibility.

## Risks / concerns

- Novel drug modality (epigenetic editing) requires FDA education and alignment; regulatory pathway not yet precedented
- Competition in FSHD space: Fulcrum Therapeutics/Sanofi oral small molecule failed Phase 3; multiple other DUX4-targeting programs active
- AAV manufacturing scale-up and capacity constraints for clinical-stage programs
- Durability claims for epigenetic edits (mitotic stability) rely on preclinical data; clinical confirmation needed
- Reliance on Stanford exclusive license for CasMINI; no indication of perpetual rights if underlying IP environment changes
- Early clinical readout (expected early 2026) critical; any safety/efficacy surprises could impact pipeline and funding trajectory
- Limited disclosed commercial partnerships beyond Kite Pharma collaboration
