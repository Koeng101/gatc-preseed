# Abalone Bio

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `antibody` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: easy · HQ: Emeryville, United States · Founded: 2018

> AI-powered antibody discovery platform using engineered yeast cells to measure functional activity of hundreds of millions of antibody variants for challenging GPCR targets.

## What they do

Abalone Bio develops a proprietary Functional Antibody Selection Technology (FAST) platform that uses engineered yeast cells to measure the functional activity of hundreds of millions of antibody variants simultaneously. The platform enables discovery and AI-guided design of target-activating antibodies, particularly for G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs), ion channels, and other membrane proteins that are difficult to drug with traditional methods.

**AI role**: Protein large language models trained on large-scale activity datasets generated from FAST campaigns to predict and design novel, functionally active antibody sequences. The company uses transformer-based models to learn sequence-function relationships and generate optimized antibody variants.

## Recommended pitch

Abalone Bio's FAST platform generates thousands of antibody variants per discovery cycle—variants that need rapid, cost-effective synthesis and validation before yeast screening. We offer a complementary solution: (1) **Clonal DNA synthesis** at 50/reaction—half the cost of IDT/Twist—for your variant libraries, positive/negative controls, and lead candidates (targeting 10k-50k constructs/year); (2) **E. coli expression with BLI throughput** for rapid Kd ranking of ML-designed hits and yeast-identified leads, reducing iteration time and cost; (3) **End-to-end binder workflow** for your designed variant batches—synthesis → expression → characterization in 2-3 week turnaround, enabling faster design-build-test cycles. Our pricing and throughput align with discovery-stage velocity. Can we discuss your current bottlenecks in construct synthesis and biophysical validation?

## Risks / concerns

- Loss of co-founder Gustavo Pesce in 2021 (skiing accident) removed key scientific visionary; company recovery dependent on retaining talent and maintaining technical momentum under Richard Yu's leadership
- Platform unproven at commercial scale: therapeutics still preclinical; no IND filings or clinical data yet published
- Competitive pressure from other functional antibody discovery platforms (Nabla Bio, others) and AI protein design companies (Absci, Ginkgo, etc.)
- GPCR agonists remain challenging therapeutics with potential on-target/off-target safety risks; path to clinical success uncertain
- Early-stage funding (~$2.15M total) limits resources; additional capital needed for clinical development and manufacturing scale-up
- Reliance on SBIR/NSF grants for non-dilutive funding; traditional venture funding rounds have been limited in scope and frequency
- Dependency on NGS cost reductions and synthetic biology maturation for platform economics; future scaling contingent on cost curves
