# Mammoth Biosciences

**Score: 3/5** · (Tier 3 — Cold) · `CRISPR_or_guide` · Stage: series_C_plus · Outreach: hard · HQ: Brisbane, United States · Founded: 2017

> Develops ultracompact CRISPR nucleases (NanoCas, CasPhi, Cas14) for in vivo gene-editing therapeutics and CRISPR diagnostics, enabling single-AAV delivery and diverse editing modalities.

## What they do

Mammoth leverages proprietary metagenomics-based protein discovery to identify and engineer compact CRISPR-Cas enzymes (one-third to one-half the size of Cas9) suitable for viral and non-viral in vivo delivery. The company develops both therapeutic in vivo gene-editing programs (lead: MB-111 for hyperlipidemias) and diagnostic platforms (DETECTR) using these compact nucleases for base editing, gene writing, epigenetic editing, and other modalities.

**AI role**: AI enabled discovery and development of novel CRISPR systems; metagenomics-based protein discovery platform to screen and identify compact CRISPR variants from natural diversity.

## Why this score

Strong fit on clonal DNA and E. coli expression services (modality match, high construct volume). However: (1) Late-stage company with established vendor relationships and in-house capabilities (protein engineering, mammalian cell line work via Horizon partnership). (2) Procurement friction high—large company procurement team unlikely to switch vendors without major service gap or cost breakthrough. (3) No in-house DNA synthesis (not a blocker, but indicates delegation to vendors). (4) Primary value prop (short DNA assembly + E. coli expression) addresses ~30–40% of their bench needs; cell-free and binder services low-fit. (5) Founder scientists (Harrington, Chen) strong technical credibility—may have entrenched supplier relationships from Berkeley days.

## Risks / concerns

- Delivery and manufacturing scale-up: LNP encapsulation and IV delivery for in vivo therapeutics is capital-intensive; dependency on third-party LNP manufacturers (e.g., Arcturus, Acuitas) may create bottleneck.
- Patent landscape: Cas9/Cas12 and broader CRISPR space is crowded (CRISPR Therapeutics, Editas, Intellia); Mammoth's non-Cas9 IP differentiation is strong but may face litigation or licensing friction.
- Clinical trial execution: MB-111 moving into IND stage 2025–26; metabolic liver disease (hypertriglyceridemia) is small market; proof-of-concept in humans critical for valuation and follow-on financing.
- Competitive pressure: CRISPR Therapeutics, Editas, and Intellia have larger funding pools and partnerships; other compact CRISPR players (e.g., Scribe Therapeutics with Type II-A systems) may accelerate in vivo programs.
- Revenue and cash burn: Diagnostic business (DETECTR) generates ~$40M revenue (2022 data), but therapeutic pipeline still preclinical; capital requirements for IND preparation and early clinical trials likely consume significant cash before any therapeutic revenue.
