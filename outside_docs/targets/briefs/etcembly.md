# Etcembly

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `miniprotein_or_nanobody` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: easy · HQ: Oxford, United Kingdom · Founded: 2020

> AI-biology company using generative LLMs to design picomolar-affinity TCR therapeutics and antibodies in months vs. years

## What they do

Etcembly develops EMLy™, a proprietary generative AI and machine learning platform that scans hundreds of millions of T-cell receptor (TCR) and antibody sequences to discover and engineer novel immunotherapies with unprecedented affinity and selectivity. The platform uses large language models (LLMs) coupled with structural modeling and bioinformatics to compress TCR discovery timelines from 2+ years to 6-11 months. Their lead program ETC-101 is a bispecific T-cell engager targeting PRAME with single-digit picomolar affinity.

**AI role**: Core to pipeline generation and optimization. EMLy uses fine-tuned LLMs with agent-based architecture to predict TCR-antigen-HLA structures, infer sequences from structures, design variants, and optimize for affinity and cross-reactivity elimination. The platform integrates proprietary in-house TCR repertoire data (including a 'long-term cancer survivor' immune dataset) with public sequences.

## Risks / concerns

- Pre-clinical stage pipeline: no clinical data yet; ETC-101 planned for IND/clinical trials in 2025 but timelines uncertain
- Limited disclosed funding: only $15.1M raised to date across all rounds; appears seed-stage funded despite advanced technical achievements, limiting runway for two pipeline programs toward clinic
- Regulatory risk on novel modality: bispecific TCR engagers are relatively new class; pathway to approval unclear relative to monoclonal antibodies or CAR-T
- Cross-reactivity elimination claim not yet clinically validated: picomolar affinity achieved in vitro; off-target binding and safety unknown in vivo
- Intellectual property landscape: TCR engineering and LLM protein design are crowded spaces; freedom-to-operate unclear on generative AI methods for immune repertoire mining
- Talent acquisition and retention: need for rare combination of TCR immunologists, ML engineers, and structural biologists; competing with well-funded AI and Big Pharma groups
- Partner dependency risk: recent DJS/AbbVie partnership is critical validation but creates execution risk if pharma collaboration underperforms or changes focus
- Data privacy/ethics: 'long-term cancer survivor study' immune dataset likely subject to regulatory scrutiny and patient consent requirements; potential delays or restrictions on data access
- Commercialization unproven: EMLy Co-pilot is newly launched; market adoption by external biotech teams remains unvalidated; revenue model unclear
- Competitive landscape: Immunocore, T-Cypher, Engimmune, and other TCR-focused biotechs are advancing; generative AI for protein design (AbInitio, DeepMind, others) rapidly evolving
