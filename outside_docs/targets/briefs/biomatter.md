# Biomatter

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `enzyme` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: easy · HQ: Vilnius, Lithuania · Founded: 2018

> <cite index="3-6">Intelligent Architecture™ platform stands at the forefront of enzyme design, enabling the creation of entirely new enzymes without the limitations of natural templates.</cite>

## What they do

<cite index="3-1,3-11">Unlike traditional approaches that rely on modifying natural enzymes, Biomatter's platform designs enzymes from the ground up, starting with the desired characteristics and building the enzyme de novo.</cite> <cite index="3-17">The platform has reduced the enzyme development cycle from years to mere weeks, delivering unique solutions for companies such as Thermo Fisher Scientific, BASF, and Neogen.</cite>

**AI role**: <cite index="4-12,4-13">Starting in 2019, Biomatter pioneered the use of generative AI to expand the protein sequence space by developing ProteinGAN, a specialized variant of the generative adversarial network that can learn natural protein sequence diversity and enable the generation of functional protein sequences. Since then, Biomatter has perfected proprietary generative AI tools and physics engines to design enzymes from the ground up, moving beyond the constraints of nature's blueprints.</cite>

## Risks / concerns

- Enzyme design AI remains unproven at manufacturing scale; most customer wins are still recent (<24 months) and lack transparent ROI/cost metrics.
- Platform is pre-product stage despite €6.5M seed raise; reliance on large industrial partners (BASF, Kirin, Neogen) could create dependency risk if validation fails.
- Founders are young scientists with limited enterprise BD/commercial experience; three out of five founders under 30 may pose execution and scaling risks.
- Competitive landscape includes established AI protein-design platforms (Absci, Ginkgo, ProteinLabs, Aqemia); differentiation via 'physics + AI' is claimed but not yet proven superior in head-to-head benchmarks.
- DNA synthesis and expression outsourcing strategy means potential vendor dependency; no clear commitment to in-house capability or long-term supply chain resilience.

## Relevant papers

- [Expanding functional protein sequence spaces using generative adversarial networks](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-021-00310-5) — Aleksej Zelezniak, Nature Machine Intelligence (2021)  
  _<cite index="42-23,42-24,42-25">Develops ProteinGAN, a self-attention-based variant of the generative adversarial network that is able to 'learn' natural protein sequence diversity and enables the generation of functional protein sequences. ProteinGAN learns the evolutionary relationships of protein sequences directly from the complex multidimensional amino-acid sequence space and creates new, highly diverse sequence variants with natural-like physical properties. Using malate dehydrogenase (MDH) as a template enzyme, 24% of the ProteinGAN-generated and experimentally tested sequences are soluble and display MDH catalytic activity.</cite>_
