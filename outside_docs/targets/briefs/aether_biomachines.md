# Aether Biomachines

**Score: 4/5** · (Tier 2 — Warm) · `enzyme` · Stage: seed_to_series_A · Outreach: moderate · HQ: Menlo Park, USA · Founded: 2017

> AI-designed protein platform that engineers molecular assemblers for industrial materials, mineral extraction, and chemical manufacturing.

## What they do

Aether combines proprietary AI (Protein Function Model) with high-throughput robotic screening to design novel proteins that act as 'molecular assemblers.' The platform is trained on proprietary datasets generated by running millions of micro-scale experiments. End applications span advanced materials (polymer additives for 3D printing), critical mineral extraction (rare earths, lithium), carbon capture, pharmaceutical synthesis, plastic recycling, and PFOA degradation.

**AI role**: Core: proprietary Protein Function Model (function-conditioned transformer) that generates protein sequences from desired industrial functions. AI enables rapid design cycle—the company tests ~300 proteins/week with 7,000+ functional readouts feeding continuous learning loop. AI is trained on in-house proprietary datasets only; not on public databases.

## Recommended pitch

Aether tests 300 proteins/week at scale. Each new sequence requires DNA synthesis—short, iterative, high-volume. Your clonal DNA assembly ($50/reaction, <2 kb) is 50% cheaper than Twist/IDT/GenScript and faster for their HTP variant-screening workflow. Offer to pilot on their next protein design cycle with a small batch (100–500 constructs). Showcase your DNA synthesis → immediate E. coli expression handoff model (even if you can't do all expression, fast DNA + fast delivery = faster iteration). Mention your flexibility to scale as they scale production (current ~25 headcount; hiring suggests rapid growth). Position as the consumable-cost lever for their platform economics—every 10% savings on DNA synthesis scales their design-to-test cycle. Pitch Jay Zaveri (President) on operational efficiency; he's an operator/investor, not a scientist, so speak to cost, speed, and supply-chain stability.

## Risks / concerns

- Early-stage revenue: Company's commercial revenue model not disclosed; RapidPrint traction is anecdotal (aerospace/defense adoption, government labs) but total revenue/ARR unknown.
- Scale of manufacturing: Aether designs novel proteins but must scale production to commodity scales for industrial partners. Transition from discovery/pilot to high-volume manufacturing is capital-intensive and typically requires partnerships or capital.
- Regulatory pathway for materials: RapidPrint is a material/additive (not a drug), but aerospace/defense qualification and supply-chain integration with OEMs/primes is slow and rigid. Long procurement cycles may limit near-term commercialization speed.
- IP/IP freedom: Company claims 'entirely proprietary' dataset. If competitors (Ginkgo, Intrinsic, others) achieve similar throughputs or open-sourced large datasets, competitive moat weakens.
- Team concentration: Pavle Jeremic is sole co-founder still active; other co-founders departed. Recent appointment of Jay Zaveri as President is positive signal but adds key-person risk if transition stumbles.
- Henkel partnership: Henkel is a lead investor and partner, but terms and scope are undisclosed. Risk of over-dependence on single strategic investor.
- Adjacent competition: Ginkgo Bioworks (cell engineering), Intrinsic Bio (AI protein design), Zymergen (materials via biology) are all in overlapping spaces. Zymergen's collapse is a cautionary tale for 'biology for manufacturing' thesis.
